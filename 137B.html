我叫陈志远，今年38，在镇江京口区开了个五金店。不大，够吃饭。

我老婆叫王雨，34岁，在一家外贸公司做会计。7月31号晚上她坐G511去上海，8月1号上午那边有个培训。她说就两天，周五回来。走之前她让我把冰箱里那条鲈鱼留着，说回来蒸。

8月1号凌晨1点19分，她给我发了一条语音，3秒钟。

我到现在听了不下两百遍。前面是很多人一起在喊，听不清喊什么；中间有"咚咚咚"的声音，像是有人在拍门；最后半秒，我听见她说"志远"。

就这两个字。后面没有了。

1点23我打过去，无法接通。1点40再打，关机。

我开车从镇江过去，走312国道，到宝华山那边被拦下来了。穿反光背心的跟我说前面线路检修，不让过。

我说我老婆在G511上。

他看了我一眼，说："哪趟车？没有车。"

他跟我说没有车。

天亮以后新闻就出来了。106人送医，疑似通风故障引发的"急性心理性反应"，情况稳定。

稳定。这两个字我看了三遍。

我给镇江一院打电话，说没有这批病人。给丹阳人民医院打，也说没有。给120打，接电话的姑娘人挺好，她说当晚确实派了9辆车过去，但人不是她们拉走的，是被"上面的车"接走的，她们的车是空着回来的。

8月1号下午，我在市卫生局门口站到天黑。有人出来，给了我一张纸，上面印着一个电话号码，让我打。

我打了。那边说："您好，您的信息已登记，请耐心等待通知。"

我问我老婆在哪家医院。他说："您好，您的信息已登记，请耐心等待通知。"

我又问了一遍。他还是那句。

8月2号，她的手机通了。响了两声，一个男的接的，没说话。我喊"你是谁"，他就挂了。再打，关机。

我侄子帮我查了定位。她手机8月2号晚上8点多在句容往南出现过一次，两分钟就没了。那个地方地图上没有名字。

我不闹。我也不信那些乱七八糟的说法。

我只想知道三件事：

她在哪个医院。
她现在怎么样。
我什么时候能见她。

念念今年五岁。她每天问一遍：妈妈什么时候回来。

我说快了，妈妈出差。

昨天她自己拿了一双小拖鞋，摆在她妈妈那双旁边。她妈妈那双粉色的，我三天没动过，鞋头朝里，跟她平时摆的一模一样。念念说，这样妈妈一进门就能看见我们。

等她回来，我第一件事是把鲈鱼蒸了。那条已经臭了，我前天扔的，但我还会再买一条，买活的。

她说想吃红烧肉。我三年没做过，嫌费事。等她回来我做，做一锅，让她吃够。

我从8月1号到现在一根烟没抽。等她回来我跟她说，你看，我戒了。

如果有谁知道一点消息，求你告诉我。哪怕就一句"她还在"，我就够了。

我的电话：138 0528 4471。我知道写了会被删，但我还是要写。

我不删帖。删了我再发。封号了我再注册。

我就在镇江。我哪儿也不去。

My name is Chen Zhiyuan. I'm 38. I run a small hardware shop in Jingkou District, Zhenjiang. It's not big. It's enough to eat.

My wife is Wang Yu. She's 34, an accountant at an import-export company. On the night of 31 July she took G511 to Shanghai — there was a training session there on the morning of 1 August. She said two days. She'd be back Friday. Before she left she told me to keep the bass in the fridge. She'd steam it when she got home.

At 1:19 a.m. on 1 August she sent me a voice message. Three seconds.

I've listened to it more than two hundred times now. At the front there are a lot of people shouting together — I can't make out the words. In the middle there's a thump, thump, thump, like someone hitting a door. And in the last half-second, I hear her say "Zhiyuan."

Just those two words. Then nothing.

At 1:23 I called. Could not connect. At 1:40 I called again. Switched off.

I drove from Zhenjiang along the 312 national road. They stopped me near Baohua Mountain. The man in the reflective vest said the line was under maintenance ahead and I couldn't pass.

I said my wife was on G511.

He looked at me and said: "Which train? There's no train."

He told me there was no train.

After dawn the news came out. 106 people hospitalised. Suspected ventilation fault causing an "acute psychogenic reaction." Condition stable.

Stable. I read those two characters three times.

I called the First People's Hospital in Zhenjiang. They said they didn't have that group of patients. I called Danyang People's Hospital. Same answer. I called 120 — the young woman on the line was decent about it. She said they really did send nine ambulances out that night, but the patients weren't taken away by them. They were taken by "the vans from above." Their own vehicles came back empty.

On the afternoon of 1 August I stood outside the Municipal Health Bureau until it got dark. Someone came out and handed me a slip of paper with a phone number printed on it and told me to call it.

I called. They said: "Hello. Your information has been registered. Please wait patiently for notification."

I asked which hospital my wife was in. They said: "Hello. Your information has been registered. Please wait patiently for notification."

I asked again. The same sentence.

On 2 August her phone rang through. It rang twice, a man answered, and said nothing. I shouted "Who is this?" He hung up. I called back. Switched off.

My nephew checked the location for me. Her phone appeared once, a little after 8 p.m. on 2 August, south of Jurong. Two minutes and it was gone. That place doesn't have a name on any map.

I'm not going to make trouble. And I don't believe any of the wild theories going round.

I just want to know three things:

Which hospital is she in.
How is she.
When can I see her.

Niannian is five this year. Every day she asks me once: when is Mum coming back.

I tell her soon. Mum's away on a work trip.

Yesterday she went and got a pair of her own little slippers and put them next to her mother's. Her mother's are the pink ones. I haven't moved them in three days — toes pointing in, exactly the way she always leaves them. Niannian said: this way, the moment Mum comes in she'll see us.

When she comes back, the first thing I'm going to do is steam that bass. It's gone off already — I threw it out the day before yesterday — but I'll buy another one. Alive, this time.

She said she wanted my braised pork. I haven't made it in three years; I always said it was too much trouble. When she's back I'll make it. A whole pot. I'll let her eat her fill.

I haven't smoked a single cigarette since 1 August. When she's back I'm going to tell her: look, I quit.

If anyone knows anything at all, please tell me. Even just "she's still there" would be enough.

My number: 138 0528 4471. I know it'll get deleted if I write it. I'm writing it anyway.

I won't delete this post. Delete it and I'll post it again. Ban the account and I'll register another.

I'm in Zhenjiang. I'm not going anywhere.

我跑长途的，1号凌晨两点多，在312国道句容段见过一个车队。

六七辆银灰色的商务车，没有牌照，或者牌照用布蒙着。前面有警车开道。我靠边停着让了一下，前后大概二十秒就过去了。

我当时还骂了一句，什么大人物半夜走这条路。

现在想想，时间对得上。

I drive long-haul. Just after 2 a.m. on the 1st I saw a convoy on the 312 national road, in the Jurong section.

Six or seven silver-grey MPVs. No plates — or plates covered with cloth. A police car running ahead of them. I pulled over to let them past. Twenty seconds, maybe, and they were gone.

At the time I cursed at them. What kind of big shot drives this road at midnight.

Thinking about it now — the timing matches.
