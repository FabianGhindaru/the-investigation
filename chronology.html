Chronology

Record of Founders and Deans

Chapter I: The Origins

c. 1090 · Burgundy, France

Brother Anseau of Vézelay

The First Documented Witness

A scribe in the scriptorium of the Benedictine abbey at Vézelay. While compiling tithe rolls for monastic estates, he documented occurrences that church doctrine could not reconcile, notably an unmapped settlement where burials refused to decay and a mountain spring whose water caused total retrograde amnesia. Dismissed by his abbot, he kept private records on sheepskin sheets titled the Liber Vulnerum (Book of Wounds). He died of winter fever in 1114; the manuscript survived in cellar storage.

Primary Artifact: The Liber Vulnerum, the oldest surviving document in Company archives. His observations matched independent records discovered in northern Italy from the same decade.

c. 1348 · Avignon and Lyon, France

Maître Raimon Aubanel

The Network's Organizer

A municipal physician during the Black Death in southern France. While inspecting mass graves, he observed physical anomalies that could not be attributed to pestilence, specifically localized drops in ground temperature and strange mineral crusts forming on fresh corpses. In 1352, he bought the Liber Vulnerum from a depopulated Burgundian monastery and linked surviving surgeons, magistrates, and notaries across Western Europe through ciphered courier routes. Chronic lung damage cut his career short, but his courier system held together.

Operating Principle: Isolate the public panic to isolate the anomaly.

Chapter II: The Council of Deans

c. 1490 · Florence, Italy

Dean Ginevra Baldesi

Financial Independence

The widow of a Florentine wool merchant who took over his workshop and banking ties. She established private trusts (fedecommessi) disguised as family investments to fund couriers and equipment, and used international art transport as legal cover for moving sensitive relics.

Contribution: Established that operations must remain self-funded and financially autonomous.

c. 1560 · Lisbon, Portugal

Dean Lourenço Brás de Menezes

Global Scope

A chart-maker attached to the Casa da Índia who realized oceanic voyages were running into events beyond European waters. He intercepted ship manifests, altered public charts to keep commercial traffic away from dangerous routes, and drafted the first index of anomalies overseas.

Contribution: Proved that containment required global coordination rather than regional management.

c. 1700 · Oxfordshire, England

Dean Lady Honor Fettiplace

Empirical Method

A widowed natural philosopher who ran a private laboratory at her late husband's manor. Corresponding under initials through male intermediaries, she replaced demonological folklore with empirical methods: control samples, standardized measurements, and repeatable trials.

Standard: Codified the baseline investigative method: observe, record, verify, and isolate.

c. 1790 · Paris, France

Dean Hyacinthe Rouvière

Security and Compartmentation

A political pamphleteer whose radicalism brought the network close to collapse. In 1790, he circulated an internal manifesto demanding all files be released to the National Constituent Assembly for public study. The council expelled him, prompting strict security and compartmentation protocols that remain in force today.

Precedent: Total ideological exposure destabilizes both the organization and civil order.

c. 1830 · Birmingham, England

Dean Josiah Fothergill

Industrial Containment

An iron founder who approached containment as an engineering problem. He fabricated the first lead-lined vaults, standardized iron transport cases, and replaced bound ledgers with indexed slip catalogues.

Contribution: Turned physical containment into a technical engineering discipline.

c. 1880 · London, England

Dean Louisa Penhallow

Administrative Architecture

A senior clerk from the British General Post Office. She introduced standardized index cards, triplicate incident logs, mandatory security vetting, and archival retention policies designed to protect documents against brittle wood-pulp degradation.

Contribution: Built the administrative framework that turned an amateur circle into a disciplined service.

c. 1931 · Lagos and London

Dean Folake da Rocha

Decentralized Logistics

A logistics manager from the Lagos Afro-Brazilian merchant community. Following the 1929 financial collapse, she decentralized network assets into autonomous safehouses across West Africa, the Americas, and Southeast Asia to prevent financial ruin.

Contribution: Created a distributed operational model capable of surviving continental collapses.

c. 1943 · Bern and Stockholm

Dean Ulrich Anderegg

Wartime Non-Alignment

Operating from neutral Switzerland during World War II, Anderegg blocked attempts by Allied and Axis intelligence units to seize artifacts, codifying permanent neutrality and severing contact with field cells loyal to national armies.

Doctrine: No sovereign state may ever command or weaponize a contained anomaly.

c. 1972 · Paris and Geneva

Dean Aurélien Faivre

The Final Dean

Recognized that private amateur networks could not evade post-war radar, wiretaps, and electronic intelligence. He opened backchannel discussions in Geneva with officials on both sides of the Iron Curtain, preparing the network for legal incorporation.

Conclusion: The informal council model had to end in order to survive modern state surveillance.

Chapter III: The Founding

1980 · Geneva, Switzerland

Dr. Amédée Rochat

Consolidation Director and First Chancellor

An archival historian who spent twenty years cataloging the fragmented records left by the Deans. With private storage leases lapsing and modern civil infrastructure encroaching on historical vaults, Rochat spent three years negotiating bilateral memoranda with NATO, Warsaw Pact, and non-aligned states. In October 1980, he incorporated the network as a Swiss-law foundation.

Institutional Charter: Independent endowment, host-state agreement in Geneva, and extraterritorial immunity via confidential bilateral memoranda.

Chapter IV: Modern Operations

2004–2014 · Central Operations

Evelyn Sinclair

Director of Administration

Led a ten-year operational overhaul. Sinclair digitized decaying physical archives into air-gapped data banks, transferred centuries of original parchment to climate-controlled vaults, replaced obsolete regional holding cells with purpose-built sites, and introduced automated surveillance monitoring for digital telecommunication lines.

Current Status: Replaced physical paper bottlenecks with secure digital architecture.
