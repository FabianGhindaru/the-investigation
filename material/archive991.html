Archivist, 28, Found Dead in Bed

Coroner rules suicide after autopsy finds no wound, no poison, and no cause

By E. Halvorsen · Chronicle Staff

MANCHESTER, Oct. 7 — Julian Mercer, a twenty-eight-year-old archivist employed by the county records office, was found dead in his bed at his apartment on Harrow Street shortly after nine o’clock Monday morning, the coroner’s office said.

He had not reported for work. A colleague who let herself in with the building key found him in the rear bedroom, the covers drawn up to his chest and his arms folded over them.

On Tuesday the death was officially ruled a suicide. Forensic investigators found no defensive wounds, no intoxicants in his blood, and no organic trauma.

By all accounts, Mr. Mercer had lain down, crossed his arms, and stopped breathing.

“An impossible, voluntary cessation of autonomic brainstem function.”

Medical examiner’s report, Oct. 8, 1991

The medical examiner’s office gave that phrase, which appears nowhere in the standard pathology literature, as the cause of death. Asked to elaborate, a spokesperson for the coroner said only that the report “stands as written.”

“His body had simply given up the will to live,” one examiner said, speaking on condition of anonymity.

No note was found. The bed was made. His shoes had been placed together on the floor beside it, toes to the wall.

Mr. Mercer had worked at the records office for six years, most recently in the basement holdings, cataloguing estate papers and unclaimed correspondence. Co-workers described him as quiet and punctual. “He was the kind of person who put things back exactly where he found them,” said Marguerite Doyle, a supervisor.

Funeral arrangements were not announced. “We don’t have a box for this,” the coroner said.
